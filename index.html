Why no interval sessions. A marathon is run almost entirely on aerobic energy, so this block builds that engine: easy volume, the long run, threshold work and marathon pace. VO2max intervals cost more recovery than they return at four runs a week, and the 5km speed you already have is not what limits a five hour marathon.

Keep your zones on Manual. Watch: Settings, Workout, Heart Rate Zones, Manual. Zone 1 upper 140. Zone 2, 141 to 155. Zone 3, 156 to 170. Zone 4, 171 to 184. Zone 5 lower 185. Left on Automatic, the watch re-estimates your max heart rate on the first of every month and your Zone 2 moves under you. Check it now.

Heart rate leads the easy runs. Effort leads the threshold sessions. Pace leads the marathon pace blocks. The wrist sensor lags 20 to 30 seconds when effort changes sharply, so on cruise intervals run by effort and read the zones afterwards. In a marathon pace block the pace is the target and heart rate is only a check.

Walking is part of the method. In the early weeks, holding Zone 2 will sometimes need walk breaks. Take them the moment the number climbs past 155 and run again when it settles. Give each run five minutes before you judge anything, heart rate always lags effort at the start.

Why The Longest Run Stops At 21 km

Plenty of marathon plans peak at 30 km. This one does not, deliberately. The strongest evidence on running injury (Frandsen 2025, 5,205 runners) says the risk that matters is a single session more than about 10% longer than your longest run in the previous 30 days. You start this block with an 8 km long run, so a 30 km peak would need a string of those spikes. The ski trip also costs a week of progression, because there is no long run on 16 January or the week after.

21 km, about 2 hours 45, is a half marathon in training with 7 km of it at marathon pace. The last 10 km of Rome will still be new ground, which is what the pacing plan and the fuelling plan are for. A plan that gets you to the start healthy beats one that looks bigger on paper.

The Intensity Split

Seiler's 80/20 distribution counts sessions, not minutes. At four runs a week that is three easy and one quality. From Week 13 the quality session is the long run itself, with marathon pace inside it, so the midweek run becomes easy running with strides.

Quality formats rotate on purpose: hills, fartlek, a progression run, cruise intervals, a checkpoint, marathon pace. Foster's 1998 work found that low variation in training load predicts illness and overreaching independently of how much you do.

The gut is trainable. Someone who has never taken 50 g an hour in training cannot do it on race day without risking stomach trouble (Jeukendrup 2014). If a gel upsets you, change the product or the timing now, not in Rome.

Race Day Fuelling

Target 45 to 60 g of carbohydrate an hour. A 25 g gel every 30 minutes from minute 20 gives 50 g an hour. Take each with water and sip sports drink at the aid stations by thirst, not by schedule. Fuel early: by the time you feel low, the gap cannot be made up. Use the exact gels you trained with, and check the official course guide for what is on offer so you know what you can leave at home.

Carbohydrate Loading And Race Week

  • Days 7 to 4 out: normal eating.
  • Thursday 11 to Saturday 13 March: carbohydrate at every meal and snack, with fibre kept low. The standard guideline for events over 90 minutes is 10 to 12 g of carbohydrate per kg of body weight a day for the last 36 to 48 hours (Burke 2011). Most people fall well short of that, so treat it as something to approach: white rice, pasta, bread, bagels, potatoes, juice.
  • Loading earlier than three days out, or only on the night before, does not work.
  • Day before: familiar food only, no new restaurant, no alcohol, drink to pale yellow.
  • Race morning: breakfast 3 to 4 hours before the start, familiar and low in fat. Porridge with honey, white toast with jam, a bagel, a banana.

Hydration And Caffeine

  • Drink to thirst. Overdrinking is more dangerous than slightly underdrinking in a marathon (Noakes). Do not try to catch up if you miss a station.
  • Sports drink over plain water for sodium. Slower runners on the course for four hours or more are the main risk group for low blood sodium: headache, nausea, confusion or swollen hands mean tell a medic.
  • Caffeine: if you use it, a normal coffee or a caffeine gel 30 to 60 minutes before the start. Rehearse it on a long run. Do not start on race day, and know that daily caffeine blunts the effect.

Iron

Ask your GP for a ferritin test (iron stores, not just haemoglobin) at some point before Christmas. Female runners and higher-mileage runners are the main risk group, and low ferritin produces exactly the flat, heavy-legged feeling that gets misread as being unfit. Do not supplement without a result.

The Stitch Problem

  • Leave 2 to 3 hours after a full meal before running. This is the biggest single fix.
  • Avoid large volumes of fluid just before, especially fizzy or sugary drinks. Sip, do not gulp.
  • Breathe into the belly, not the chest.
  • If one arrives: slow down, press into the spot and exhale hard as the opposite foot lands.
  • Long game: stitches get rarer as aerobic fitness improves and breathing settles.

Watch Setup

  • Keep heart rate zones on Manual with the numbers on the Paces tab. Automatic zones re-estimate your max heart rate every month, which for someone who has not raced flat out means a low estimate and a Zone 2 that quietly moves.
  • Build the quality sessions as Custom Workouts. Workout app, Outdoor Run, Create Workout, Custom. Add a warmup, repeating work and recovery intervals by time or distance, and a cooldown. For marathon pace blocks add a pace alert of 6:50–7:00/km on the work step. The spreadsheet that came with this plan lists every workout in exactly that structure.
  • Cold mornings: in winter the wrist sensor sometimes locks onto your cadence instead of your pulse and reports a believable but wrong number. If it sits oddly flat or matches your step rate, ignore it for that run and use the talk test. A sleeve pulled over the watch helps.

Easy and long runs need none of this. Start an Outdoor Run, turn the Digital Crown to the Heart Rate Zone view and keep it on Zone 2.

Moving Sessions: Uni, Christmas And The Ski Trip

The skeleton is Monday easy, Wednesday quality, Thursday easy (from 3 December) and Saturday long. It is a shape, not a rule. Keep a rest day before the long run, and never run three days in a row.

  • 11 to 11 work days are rest days. Do not squeeze a run around a twelve hour shift on your feet.
  • Christmas, Week 8 (21 to 27 Dec): lighter by design. Move any session to whichever day works.
  • New Year, Week 9: if Wednesday does not work, Thursday does, but drop that Thursday's easy run.
  • Ski trip, Thursday 14 to Friday 22 January: Week 11 finishes on Wednesday 13th. Skiing all day is real training, long hours on your feet and heavy on the quads. If a safe, ice-free route turns up, an easy 20 to 30 minute jog is plenty, and doing none is fine. Saturday 23rd is a gentle 8km return run, then Week 13 picks the long run back up at 16.5km.
  • Winter dark: many Monday, Wednesday and Thursday runs will be after dark. Lit routes and something reflective, and keep Saturday's long run for daylight.

Daily Flags

  • Resting HR 5+ bpm above your normal 54 for two mornings running: downgrade the day to easy. No quality. Health app, Browse, Heart, Resting Heart Rate.
  • Resting HR 8+ bpm above baseline: full rest. Reassess tomorrow.
  • Knee pain, particularly on the outside or under the kneecap: stop the session. Two days off, and a physio before you run again if it is still there.
  • Under 5 hours sleep: quality becomes easy.
  • Flat and achy three days in a row: take an unplanned deload and repeat the previous week.
  • Bone pain gets no benefit of the doubt. Tenderness when you press on a bone that gets worse through a run means stop and get it imaged.
  • Speak to a GP if your period becomes irregular or stops, if you are ill repeatedly, or if fatigue does not respond to rest days. These are training-load signals, not badges.

Session Signals

  • Easy runs coming back mostly Zone 3 or 4: too fast. Slow down or add walk breaks. Open the run in the Fitness app, tap the heart rate graph and look at time in each zone.
  • Cannot hold the cruise reps at a steady effort: ease off. A rep 10 seconds slower is still a threshold rep. A rep you blow up in is not.
  • Walking during the recovery jogs: the session was pitched too hard.
  • Marathon pace feels hard inside the first 3km of a block: the pace is wrong for you today, or the legs are tired. Ease off and tell me.
  • Heart rate drifting up in the last 30 to 40 minutes of a long run at constant pace: normal. Do not fight it.
  • Pace at the same easy heart rate has not improved by Week 13: tell me. Usually the easy days are too hard, occasionally it is iron.
  • Three poor quality sessions in a row: a mandatory deload, not something to push through.

Missed Sessions

Do not stack and do not chase. The next session is what was already planned, not the missed one bolted on.

  • Missed easy run: skip it.
  • Missed quality: shift it a day if there is room, otherwise skip.
  • Missed long run: shift a day or two, or cut it by a quarter if it is late in the week. In Weeks 13 to 16 tell me, because the marathon pace blocks build week on week.
  • Two to four days off: come back at about 75% and skip the next quality session.
  • Five to seven days off: rebuild over the following week, quality returns the week after.

Consistency beats completion. One missed run or one bad week does not ruin a marathon block. Consistency over months does the work.

If You Are Ill In Race Week

Mild cold, head only, no fever: you run.

Chest, fever, or aching all over: do not start. A missed marathon is a bad weekend. Running 42km with a chest infection is a genuine risk.
