Do this before your first run: set your zones to Manual. Left on Automatic, your watch estimates your max heart rate from your own workout data and re-does the sums on the first of every month. Since you have never run flat out, that estimate will be low, your Zone 2 will sit too low with it, and the boundary will move under you mid-block. Set it once and leave it.

Watch: Settings, Workout, Heart Rate Zones, Manual. Or on the phone: Watch app, My Watch, Workout, Heart Rate Zones, Manual. Zone 1 takes an upper limit only, Zones 2 to 4 take both, Zone 5 takes a lower limit only. Enter: Zone 1 upper 140. Zone 2, 141 to 155. Zone 3, 156 to 170. Zone 4, 171 to 184. Zone 5 lower 185. Your date of birth has to be in the Health app or zones will not calculate at all.

Heart rate leads the easy runs. Effort leads the quality sessions. On an easy run, turn the Digital Crown to the Heart Rate Zone view and simply keep it on Zone 2. On the fartlek, threshold and hill sessions, run by feel and check the zones afterwards instead. Wrist sensors lag by 20 to 30 seconds when effort changes sharply, so chasing the number mid-rep will have you running the first minute far too hard.

Walking is part of the method, not a failure. In the first few weeks, holding Zone 2 will probably require walk breaks. Take them the moment the number climbs past 155. You are not failing to run 5km, you are training the exact system that will let you run it comfortably by the end of October. Give it five minutes at the start of each run before you judge anything, heart rate always lags behind effort early on.

Honest read: you are not a beginner. Someone who can cover 10km with two short walks already has a usable aerobic base. What you do not have is volume, and volume is the single biggest lever on 5km comfort at your stage. That is what this block adds.

One caveat on the numbers. A max of 199 is plausible for 19, but unless it came from an all-out effort it is an estimate, and every zone here is built on it. If the Week 8 time trial pushes you past 199, tell me and every band shifts up. Treat these zones as a very good working model, not gospel.

What This Block Must Deliver

  • Long run: 4 km building to 8 km, all of it genuinely easy.
  • Zone 2 quality: easy running that actually sits at 141 to 155 rather than drifting into Zone 3.
  • One quality session per week. Never two. Below 60 km per week the recovery is not there for a second one.
  • Two strength sessions protecting the knee and improving how much speed you get per unit of effort.
  • A measured result: baseline 5km in Week 3, time trial in Week 8.

Why Zone 2 Is The Whole Point

You said you want 5km to feel faster and more comfortable. Those are the same problem. Right now 5km is uncomfortable because the pace you run it at sits close to your threshold, so you are clearing lactate slower than you make it and the stitch and the breathing follow.

Easy aerobic volume fixes that from underneath. Sustained work at 141 to 155 builds mitochondrial density and capillary networks, which is San Millán's case for Zone 2 and Lydiard's case for base building sixty years before the lab work existed. The effect you feel is that the same 5km happens at a lower heart rate, and then at the same heart rate but quicker.

The trade: it is slow to feel dramatic and fast to actually work. Five weeks of consistent easy volume moves the needle far more than five weeks of running every session at moderate effort, which is the trap almost every casual runner falls into.

The measurement that matters: your pace at a given heart rate. If in Week 7 you are covering more ground at 150 bpm than you were in Week 3, the block has worked, whatever the time trial says on the day.

The Intensity Split

Seiler's 80/20 distribution counts sessions, not minutes. Three runs a week means two easy and one quality, which is 67/33 on paper but closer to 85/15 by time once warm-ups and cool-downs are counted. That is the correct shape for where you are.

Quality sessions rotate deliberately across the block: hill strides, fartlek, threshold reps, progression run. This is not variety for its own sake. Foster's 1998 work on training monotony found that low day-to-day variation in training stress predicts illness and overreaching independently of how much you are doing.

Training While Cutting

You can run a fat loss phase and a training block at the same time. Plenty of people do. It requires two things: a moderate rather than aggressive deficit, and the food that is left being placed where it does the most good.

Protein: 1.8 to 2.2 g/kg, roughly 140 to 170 g a day. The high end matters more in a deficit than out of one, because protein is what stops the weight you lose coming partly from muscle.

Do not cut harder because the running is going well. Training load rises every week from Week 3 onward. If the deficit rises alongside it, the two stack and you get the classic outcome: sessions get worse, sleep gets worse, and the weight stops moving anyway. Slow is the fast way here.

Holiday, Weeks 1 and 2. Eat the food. Two weeks of eating normally on holiday will not undo anything, and the block is deliberately at its lightest then. Trying to hold a deficit abroad while starting a new training habit is a good way to make both fail.

Energy Availability · Non-Negotiable

Low energy availability is the mechanism behind RED-S, and young female distance runners are the highest risk group for it. This is not a lecture about eating enough in general, it is a specific and well documented failure mode when training load rises and intake falls at the same time.

Stop the deficit and speak to a GP if any of these show up:

  • Your period becomes irregular, lighter, or stops. This is a medical signal, never a sign of training hard enough.
  • Fatigue that a rest day does not touch.
  • Resting heart rate creeping up week on week in the Health app, or the Vitals app flagging overnight outliers repeatedly.
  • Repeated colds or infections, which is worth watching in a first term at uni anyway.
  • Any pain that is tender to press on a bone, particularly the shin or the top of the foot.

Bone pain gets no benefit of the doubt. Point tenderness on a bone that worsens through a run and does not warm up means stop running and get it imaged. Stress fractures are managed medically, not by training around them.

Framework: IOC Consensus Statement on RED-S (2014, updated 2018). Female athlete physiology per Stacy Sims, ROAR and NEXT LEVEL.

Around Sessions

  • Before an easy run: nothing needed if it is within a few hours of a meal. Do not run the long run fully fasted while in a deficit.
  • Before quality or the long run: something carb-based 1.5 to 2 hours ahead. Toast and jam, a banana, a bowl of cereal.
  • After: 25 to 30 g of protein plus carbs within a couple of hours. This is the single most protected part of the day when calories are limited.
  • Iron: worth a ferritin check with your GP at some point this block. Low ferritin is common in young female runners and produces exactly the flat, heavy-legged feeling that gets misread as being unfit.

The Stitch Problem

You mentioned needing walk breaks partly to clear stitches. Exercise-related transient abdominal pain has no single agreed cause, but the practical levers are well established and they are mostly about timing and breathing.

  • Leave 2 to 3 hours after a full meal before running. This is the biggest single fix.
  • Avoid large volumes of fluid immediately before, especially anything fizzy or high sugar. Sip rather than gulp.
  • Breathe into the belly, not the chest. Shallow chest breathing is strongly associated with it.
  • If one arrives: slow down, press into the spot, exhale hard as the opposite foot lands. Do not stop dead unless you have to.
  • Long game: the dead bugs and side planks in the strength sessions genuinely help, and stitches get rarer as aerobic fitness improves and breathing calms down.

The warm-up is not optional for a 5km. Unlike a marathon, a 5km starts near your ceiling immediately. Going in cold means the first kilometre is spent catching up on oxygen you should already have been delivering, and it costs far more than the warm-up does. In cold weather it matters more, not less.

Pacing · Even to Negative Split

First 400 to 800 m: deliberately a touch slower than what feels right. Adrenaline makes the opening feel easy and it is where every 5km is lost.

km 1 to 3.5: settle into the hardest effort you believe you can hold to the finish. Uncomfortable but rhythmic. Three or four words if someone asked you a question.

Final 1 to 1.5 km: this is where it is won. Whatever is left, spend it. Heart rate will run into the high 180s and that is exactly right.

Use the same route you used for the Week 3 baseline. Comparing a flat measured 5km against a hilly one tells you nothing about fitness.

No gels, no fuelling. A 5km is over long before glycogen becomes a factor. Caffeine is fine if you already drink it, roughly 45 to 60 minutes before, but do not introduce it on the day.

Watch Setup · Ten Minutes, Once

Two things to do before Monday, and then you can forget about them.

  • Set your heart rate zones to Manual with the numbers on the Paces tab. Automatic zones re-estimate your max heart rate every month from your own workout data, which for someone who has never raced flat out means a low estimate and a Zone 2 that quietly moves. Manual keeps the target still for eight weeks, which is the only way the checkpoints mean anything.
  • Build the quality sessions as Custom Workouts. Workout app, Outdoor Run, the three dots, Create Workout, Custom. You get a warmup, repeating work and recovery intervals by time or distance, and a cooldown, and the watch buzzes you through it. The spreadsheet that came with this plan lists every session in exactly that structure. Build the fartlek, the threshold session and the sharpener once each and they are saved for good.

The easy runs and long runs need none of this. Start an Outdoor Run, turn the Digital Crown to the Heart Rate Zone view, keep it on Zone 2. That is the entire protocol for roughly 85% of this block.

Moving Sessions · Holiday and Uni

The skeleton is Monday run plus lift, Wednesday quality, Thursday lift, Saturday long run. It is a shape, not a rule. Two things will disrupt it and both are already built in.

  • Holiday 11 to 18 Sept: this covers the back half of Week 1 and nearly all of Week 2. Both weeks are set at 10 km with three short, portable runs and one hotel gym session. Run early before the heat. If you miss one entirely, nothing is lost, the block has not really started yet.
  • Uni from 28 Sept: that is Week 4, which is also where the first quality session lands. Once you have your timetable, drag sessions onto the days that work. Hold two rules while you do it: keep 48 hours between the quality session and the long run, and never put two running days back to back.
  • 11 to 11 work days: those are rest days. Do not try to squeeze a run around a twelve hour shift on your feet.
  • Cold mornings and wrist heart rate. In cold weather optical sensors sometimes lock onto your cadence instead of your pulse and report a number that looks plausible but is not. If the reading sits oddly flat or matches your step rate, ignore it for that run and go by the talk test. A sleeve pulled over the watch helps.
  • Clocks go back Sun 25 Oct. From Week 8, evenings are dark. Move runs to daylight where you can, otherwise lit routes and something reflective. Do not run the time trial in the dark.

Daily Flags

  • RHR 5+ bpm above 54 for two mornings running: downgrade the day to easy. No quality.
  • RHR 8+ bpm above baseline: full rest. Reassess tomorrow. Health app, Browse, Heart, Resting Heart Rate.
  • Knee pain, particularly on the outside or under the kneecap: stop the session. Two days off. If it is still there, physio before you run again.
  • Under 5 hours sleep: quality becomes easy. You sleep 8 normally, so this should be rare, and first term is exactly when it stops being rare.
  • Flat and achy three days in a row: take an unplanned deload week. Repeat the previous week rather than pressing on.

Session Signals

  • Easy runs coming back mostly Zone 3 or 4: you are running easy days too hard. Slow down or add walk breaks. Open the run in the Fitness app, tap the heart rate graph, and look at time in each zone. This is the single most common way base blocks fail.
  • Cannot hold the threshold reps at a steady effort: ease off rather than fighting it. A rep run 10 seconds slower is still a threshold rep. A rep you blow up in is not.
  • Walking during the recovery jogs in a quality session: the session was pitched too hard. Take it down a notch next time.
  • Long run pace at the same heart rate has not moved by Week 7: tell me. Usually it means easy days are too hard, occasionally it means iron.
  • Three poor quality sessions in a row: that is a mandatory deload, not something to push through.

Missed Sessions

Do not stack and do not chase. The next session is whatever was already planned, not the missed one bolted on.

  • Missed easy run: skip it. It is gone and it does not matter.
  • Missed quality: shift it one day if there is room, otherwise skip.
  • Missed long run: shift a day or two, or cut it by a quarter if it is late in the week.
  • Two to four days off: come back at about 75% and skip the next quality session.
  • Five to seven days off: rebuild over the following week, quality returns the week after.

Consistency beats completion. Six weeks at 80% of this plan beats eight weeks of trying to catch up on missed mileage and getting hurt in Week 5.

If You Are Ill in Week 8

Mild cold, head only, no fever: the time trial goes ahead.

Chest, fever, or aching all over: do not time trial. Move it to the following Saturday. There is no race entry and no reason to take the risk, particularly in freshers' term when everyone is passing things around.
